# Authenticity and Quality Review

**Review purpose:** Determine whether this package resembles defensible consulting work without implying that a real engagement occurred.

## Credibility checks passed

- The company and every assessment result are conspicuously labeled fictional or simulated.
- Technology presence is separated from control status; unknown configurations remain unknown.
- Business processes, identities, data, and vendors are treated as assets—not just hardware.
- Construction-specific exposure appears in payment fraud, project closeout, subcontractor sharing, field/BYOD use, and South Florida continuity planning.
- Scope distinguishes a risk assessment from penetration testing, financial audit, legal review, compliance validation, and implementation.
- Risk scenarios identify asset/process, threat event, precondition, likelihood, impact, owner, treatment, and evidence status.
- Residual risk is not invented; it remains `Not assessed` until controls are evidenced.
- Finding language includes condition, expected practice, plausible cause to validate, risk, recommendation, and required evidence.
- Remediation closure depends on operating evidence and risk re-scoring rather than product purchase.

## What this package intentionally does not claim

- No interviews, samples, screenshots, tenant exports, logs, contracts, scans, or configuration reviews occurred.
- The findings are teaching examples, not discovered deficiencies.
- The 1–5 model is an internal prioritization method, not a NIST or CIS scoring formula.
- Framework references indicate alignment at the category/control-family level; they are not complete safeguard mappings or compliance conclusions.
- Preliminary CIA ratings, owners, RTO/RPO, financial thresholds, and due dates require management validation.

## Gates required before real issuance

1. Execute authorization and rules of engagement; confirm systems, entities, locations, and evidence-handling requirements.
2. Validate asset ownership, data flows, business-impact thresholds, and recovery tolerances with management.
3. Collect attributable evidence and record source, date, population, sample, exceptions, and reviewer.
4. Replace each simulated condition with supported observation—or remove it.
5. Obtain factual-accuracy responses and management action plans without weakening independent conclusions.
6. Recalculate risk using approved criteria and evidenced existing-control effectiveness.
7. Perform technical and editorial quality review; issue a controlled final report.
